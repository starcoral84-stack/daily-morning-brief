🔥 The big one

xAI is quietly turning Grok into infrastructure, not just a chatbot.

The experimental Grok TypeScript SDK matters more than the words “new SDK” suggest. It wraps text, voice, image and video generation, but the interesting bit is the tool layer: web search, X search, code execution and remote MCP.

That means the product direction is shifting from ask Grok a question toward build software that can send Grok out to do things. In other words, Grok is increasingly trying to become a programmable agent substrate rather than a very talkative website.

The fact that the SDK blocks browser use is also a small but important signal: xAI is treating agent apps as server-side systems with secrets, tools and permissions, not little scripts you paste into a webpage and hope for the best.

Chatbot era: “Ask me anything.”
Agent era: “Give me credentials, a toolchain and a mildly alarming amount of autonomy.”

🕳️ Rabbit hole

Remote MCP is where the plumbing starts becoming the product.

MCP sounds dull until you realise what it does: it gives an AI a standard way to talk to external tools and services. Once models can discover tools, call them and hand results back and forth, the model itself becomes only one component in a larger machine.

The interesting question is no longer simply “which model is smartest?” It becomes: which ecosystem has the best tools, permissions, memory, reliability and orchestration?

That is why OpenClaw, Grok Bot, Dots and similar systems matter. They are all competing to become the layer that decides which model acts, which tool it uses, what it remembers and what happens next.

Rabbit-hole thought: once model quality is “good enough” for many jobs, tool access and orchestration may matter more than a tiny benchmark lead. The winner could be the boring plumbing. History has a sense of humour.

🧠 Wait, that's important

Anthropic is spending $100 million training people to deploy Claude in the real world.

The Frontier Academy announcement is easy to dismiss as corporate training news. I would not. Training 10,000 “Frontier Deployed Engineers” suggests Anthropic thinks one of the bottlenecks is no longer simply model capability — it is people who can actually embed AI into messy organisations.

The named organisations are banks, consultancies and big enterprises. That points to a future where “AI engineer” increasingly means someone who understands workflows, permissions, data, tool use, human hand-offs and how not to accidentally automate the wrong thing at scale.

The next elite AI skill may be less “I know the cleverest prompt” and more “I can make this thing survive contact with Finance.”

🤖 Actually useful AI

If you are testing agents, stop judging them by one clever answer.

A useful test this week would be to give three agent systems the same recurring job and score them on boring things:

1. Does it remember what happened yesterday?
2. Does it know when to use a tool instead of hallucinating?
3. Can it recover when an API changes or fails?
4. Does it ask for help at the right moment?
5. Can you understand what it did afterwards?

That is a much better agent benchmark than “it wrote a lovely paragraph once.”

🔗 Weird connection

Grok SDK + Grok Build + Claude Frontier Academy = prompt engineering is growing up.

Today's brief contains three separate clues: xAI is packaging Grok for developers, Grok Build is reportedly adding multi-session agent dashboards, and Anthropic is training thousands of engineers to deploy AI inside organisations.

Together they point toward a shift from prompting one model to orchestrating fleets of models and tools. Multiple sessions. Isolated worktrees. External services. Persistent memory. Human review. Permissions.

The phrase “prompt engineer” may eventually sound a bit like “person who is good at Google”. Useful, yes. Entire profession? Perhaps the job moved somewhere more complicated while we were busy arguing about wording.

👀 Watch this

Platform accountability for AI-generated sexual deepfakes.

The TBIJ report on Grok is more consequential than another model launch. Police reportedly struggled to obtain account data needed to pursue people requesting sexualised deepfakes, while victims are pursuing legal routes and regulators are tightening requirements around illegal intimate imagery.

This is one to watch because the core question is bigger than Grok: when an AI system makes harmful content easy to request at scale, what obligations fall on the platform around identity, records, moderation and cooperation with investigators?

🎃 October weirdness

Tonight's film has one of the great “why does this sound alien?” instruments.

The Day the Earth Stood Still is on tonight's local listing, and its score is famous for using the theremin — an electronic instrument played without touching it. The performer moves their hands near antennas to control pitch and volume.

The result is perfect old-school science-fiction unease: a voice-like glide that seems human-adjacent but never quite settles into one. It sounds as if a violin has died, come back, and is now refusing to explain itself.

Library of Congress material specifically points to the film as a classic place to hear the theremin. If you watch it tonight, listen to how much of the alien atmosphere comes from sound before anything strange is even on screen.

1951 special effect: two antennas and a musician waving their hands near them.
Still creepier than half the CGI budget of 2026.
